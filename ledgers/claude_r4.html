How it trades

Paper test (WO-149): a $1,000 paper bankroll, no real orders; fills are simulated from what live orders got.

The open tier's signal, but checked every minute and bought steadily while the disagreement lasts, then held to settlement.

  1. Before the rain day starts, checks every market every minute, if the market's buy and sell prices are within 6c and the forecast is less than 6 hours old.
  2. When the forecast and price differ by 25 points or more, it buys the forecast's side, with a first stake that grows with the gap, up to $500.
  3. Then, every minute the gap stays 25 points or more, it buys 5 more contracts.
  4. Never sells. Every bet is held to settlement.

How to read the money: spent is what the bot paid for its contracts, including fees. P&L is what came back at settlement (or from an early sale) minus that, and the ROI beside it is P&L divided by what was spent on those same settled markets. Open markets are not in the P&L or the spend until they settle. A Kalshi contract pays $1 if its side wins and $0 if it loses. "YES" bets on rain at the airport gauge that day, "NO" bets on no rain. Live money bots come from their real Kalshi records on the box. Paper bots place no real orders: they decide on the same minute's real prices, and their fills are simulated from what our live orders actually got, so a paper fill is not a confirmed exchange fill. Anything the records cannot confirm reads unknown, never a number. No balances and no order ids are published here.
